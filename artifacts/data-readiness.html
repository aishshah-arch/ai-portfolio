The Data Readiness Playbook

Most AI projects don't fail because of the algorithm. They fail because of the data. This interactive guide walks through three data challenges that can sink a project, and what to check before each one.

Challenge 2 · Before you scale

Scaling without inheriting bias

The case: TalentBridge trained a résumé-screening model on ten years of one company's hiring decisions. It tests well on that history. Now the company wants to use it in a new country, on ten times as many applications.

The first problem is the label: the model learned "who past managers hired," not "who would succeed." A common fairness check is the four-fifths rule: each group's selection rate should be at least 80% of the highest group's. Below, a fictional model passes that check. But the recruiter only has time to interview the top of the ranked list. Move the slider to see who actually gets an interview.

Evidence to require before scaling

  • Local validation: test on applications from the new country, reviewed by local recruiters who know its schools and résumé styles.
  • Error rates by group, not just selection rates: how many qualified people in each group were wrongly rejected?
  • A limited pilot with human review of rejections before the full rollout.
  • Capacity at 10× volume: enough reviewers, appeals, and monitoring to catch problems before they reach thousands of applicants.

Challenge 3 · Before you share

Sharing without exposing patients

The case: two hospitals, Riverbend and Lakeside, want to build a better diagnostic model together, since each one's data is too small alone. The records are sensitive, the hospitals code data differently, and neither wants to hand over raw records.

One approach is federated learning: the model travels to each hospital and trains there, and only model updates are shared, not patient records. To protect patients further, teams can add differential privacy, mathematical noise that makes it harder to learn anything about one person. But noise has a cost. Move the slider.

No single technique is enough

1. Make the data compatible firstMap both hospitals to the same codes and definitions. Combining mismatched data can make a model worse, not better.
2. Remember what still leaksModel updates and the final model can still reveal information. Privacy techniques reduce risk; they don't remove it.
3. Add security and governanceAccess controls, audit logs, data-use agreements, and clear rules on who may use the model and for what.
4. Validate at each siteTest the shared model separately at both hospitals to make sure it works for each patient population.

Curves are illustrative. Real tradeoffs depend on the data, the model, and the privacy settings.
