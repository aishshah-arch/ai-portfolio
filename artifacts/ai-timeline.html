From Turing to Transformers: 75 Years of Artificial Intelligence

AI did not rise in a straight line. It moved through bursts of optimism, two long "winters," and a modern boom powered as much by GPUs, data, and cloud computing as by new algorithms.

Created by Aishwarya Shah · Indiana Wesleyan University · October 2026

1950 to 1973: The Foundations

Symbolic AI and early optimism

1950

The Turing Test

Alan Turing's paper "Computing Machinery and Intelligence" asks whether machines can think and proposes the imitation game as a test.

Alan Turing
1956

AI is born at Dartmouth

A summer workshop at Dartmouth College launches AI as a research field, using the term "artificial intelligence" coined in its 1955 proposal.

John McCarthy, Marvin Minsky, Claude Shannon, Nathaniel Rochester
1958

The Perceptron

The first trainable artificial neural network learns to classify simple patterns, the seed of today's deep learning.

Frank Rosenblatt, Cornell
1966

ELIZA, the first chatbot

A simple pattern-matching program imitates a therapist and surprises users who believe it understands them.

Joseph Weizenbaum, MIT
1969

Limits of the Perceptron

The book "Perceptrons" shows that single-layer networks cannot solve some basic problems, cooling interest in neural networks for over a decade.

Marvin Minsky and Seymour Papert

1974 to 1980: The First AI Winter

Promises outran results

1973

The Lighthill Report

A UK government review concludes AI has failed to meet its grand promises. Funding is cut in the UK, and U.S. agencies such as DARPA also scale back.

1974–80

Winter sets in

Causes: limited computing power, little data, and systems that worked only on toy problems. Impact: research funding and public interest dropped sharply for years.

1980 to 1987: Expert Systems Boom

AI goes commercial

1980

Expert systems in business

Rule-based systems like XCON at Digital Equipment Corporation capture human expertise as if-then rules and save companies millions.

Digital Equipment Corporation, Carnegie Mellon
1986

Backpropagation popularized

A landmark paper shows how multi-layer neural networks can learn from their errors, reviving neural network research.

David Rumelhart, Geoffrey Hinton, Ronald Williams

1987 to 1993: The Second AI Winter

The expert systems bubble bursts

1987–93

Second winter

Causes: the market for specialized Lisp machines collapsed, and expert systems proved expensive to maintain and brittle when rules did not fit reality. Impact: many AI companies failed, and researchers avoided the label "AI," calling their work machine learning or data mining instead.

1989 to 2011: Quiet Progress

Machine learning matures

1989

Neural networks read handwriting

Convolutional neural networks learn to recognize handwritten digits, later used to read checks at banks.

Yann LeCun, Bell Labs
Early 1990s

AI fights card fraud

Neural network systems such as Falcon begin scoring credit card transactions for fraud in real time, one of the first large-scale commercial uses of machine learning in banking.

HNC Software (later part of FICO)
1997

Deep Blue beats Kasparov

IBM's chess computer defeats the reigning world champion, a milestone for search and computing power rather than learning.

IBM
2006

Deep learning and the cloud arrive

Hinton's work on deep belief networks helps relaunch "deep learning." The same year, Amazon Web Services launches cloud storage and computing, making massive computing power rentable.

Geoffrey Hinton · Amazon Web Services
2009

ImageNet

A dataset of millions of labeled images gives researchers the data that deep networks need.

Fei-Fei Li, Princeton and Stanford
2011

Watson and Siri

IBM Watson wins Jeopardy!, and Apple launches Siri, bringing AI into everyday life.

IBM · Apple

2012 to Today: The Deep Learning and Cloud Era

Hardware, data, and algorithms finally meet

2012

The AlexNet moment

A deep neural network trained on Nvidia GPUs crushes the ImageNet competition. Hardware has finally caught up with decades of theory.

Alex Krizhevsky, Ilya Sutskever, Geoffrey Hinton · Nvidia
2014

Generative networks (GANs)

Two networks compete to create realistic images, an early step toward generative AI.

Ian Goodfellow
2016

AlphaGo beats Lee Sedol

DeepMind's system wins at Go, a game long thought too intuitive for computers.

Google DeepMind, Demis Hassabis
2017

The Transformer

The paper "Attention Is All You Need" introduces the architecture behind nearly every modern language model.

Google researchers
2020

GPT-3 and AlphaFold 2

A 175-billion-parameter language model writes fluent text, and AlphaFold 2 predicts protein structures, solving a 50-year biology challenge.

OpenAI · Google DeepMind
2022

ChatGPT goes public

Released in November 2022, ChatGPT becomes one of the fastest-growing consumer apps in history and brings generative AI to the mainstream.

OpenAI, backed by Microsoft cloud computing
2023

The model race

GPT-4, Anthropic's Claude, and Google's Gemini compete, and companies race to add AI to products.

OpenAI · Anthropic · Google
2024

Nobel Prizes and regulation

Hinton and John Hopfield win the Nobel Prize in Physics for neural network foundations, Hassabis and John Jumper share the Chemistry prize for AlphaFold, and the EU adopts the AI Act, the first major AI law.

Nobel Committee · European Union
Today

AI across finance

Banks use machine learning for fraud detection, credit risk, and customer targeting, while regulators focus on explainability and fair lending, a reminder that powerful models must also be trustworthy.

Industry and regulators

Why the boom happened now

Training compute for notable AI systems doubled roughly every 20 months before 2010. Since the deep learning era began, it has doubled about every 6 months.

~6 months

That surge, made possible by GPUs and cloud computing, explains why ideas from the 1950s and 1980s only took off after 2012 (Roser, 2022; Sevilla et al., 2022).
